What is this?

Off the Page is a short visual novel, about an hour long, made as a surprise for my forty-first birthday. It's set on the night before that birthday, in my living room in the Korean countryside, when something comes tumbling out of the painting on my wall. The rest you'll have to find out for yourself.

You play as me (or as you; it asks for your name). It's funny, it's sweet, it will very possibly make you cry, and it's stuffed with real details from my life: my kindergarten journal, the little lists of things I'm thankful for that I post every Thanksgiving, my family, my cat. It's a love letter, more or less. Addressed to me, which is a strange and lovely thing to receive.

How it was made

I write romance and fantasy, and lately I've been making visual novels with Claude, the AI from Anthropic. By now there's a whole stack of guides on how I like my stories, my art, my prose, and my code. So the day before my birthday, I asked Claude for something new: make me a visual novel, by yourself, and don't tell me anything about it.

I answered a handful of questions (my earliest memory of writing, what's outside my window in November, my favorite cake, which fictional men I'd run away with), handed over some photos and a decade of thankful lists, and then got out of the way. Claude came up with the story, wrote it, made every picture with Google's Nano Banana image model, cleaned up the art, and built the whole game in Ren'Py. It even generated the images itself, inside a project I could open but had to promise not to look at. Which I'd covered with this:

I played it on my birthday and laughed and cried in roughly equal measure. And then, because I can't not, I edited it: fixed the wobbly bits, tightened some lines, added details only I would know, lampshaded a few oddities, and wrangled the occasional robber with too many arms. About three quarters of it is still exactly as Claude first wrote it.

How to play

If you've never played a visual novel: it's a story you read, with pictures and music, where every so often you choose what happens. There are no wrong answers and no way to lose. Turn your sound on.

Next lineClick, tap, or press Space / Enter
Make a choiceClick or tap one of the buttons that appear
Go backScroll your mouse wheel up, or press Page Up. You can go back past a choice and pick something different.
Read what you missedThe History button at the bottom of the screen
Hide the text boxPress H (or middle-click) to admire the art. Press it again to bring the text back.
Skip what you've readHold Ctrl, or click Skip
Auto-advanceClick Auto
Menu, save & loadPress Esc or right-click. Saves live in your browser, so use the same browser to come back.

Best on a computer or a tablet held sideways. The first load downloads about 80 MB, so give it a minute on a slow connection. The main menu also has a gallery of the illustrations you've unlocked, a scene maker for posing the characters yourself, and the credits.

Before you start

Content note. Most of this story is warm and silly, with some swearing and some tears. But in chapter three, you'll be asked what kind of story this is:
